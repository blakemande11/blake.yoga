Lately, I've been finding unexpected solace in yin and restorative yoga practices and I've been wondering why that is. I'm coming to see yin as a practice of subtle power, an amplification of presence in a world dominated by hustle.

If you're unfamiliar, yin yoga is a much slower practice, where poses are typically held for at least three minutes. For a long time I struggled to embrace this slower practice. My mind would feel unsettled and my body full of tension. From a more practical perspective I've noticed two changes in myself here: 

> **(1) Props are tools not crutches:** I have learned to *love* using props. Blocks, bolsters, blankets, and straps aren't signs of limitation or inadequacy. If anything, I now see my willingness to use props as a sign of courage - a recognition of the benefits I can get from supporting myself. Props enable us to work from a solid foundation and help signal to our brain and body that it's safe to access deeper states of relaxation because we're not straining to hold a pose beyond our capacity. 

>**(2) Yin is more than hamstring stretches:** The second practical change I've noticed is that a yin yoga practice does not need to be focused solely on hamstring flexibility. I have perennially tight hamstrings so the thought of spending an hour exclusively in forward folds and splits is horrifying. When I am in those poses I use my props and bend my knees, but the world of yin yoga has so much more to offer beyond hamstring stretches. 

We've come to conflate collapse with rest. Hours spent "bed rotting" while scrolling on our phones masquerade as recovery, but instead often leave us overstimulated and numb. I'm certainly guilty of thinking I need a break, only to end up on my phone, leaving me more scattered than before. True rest—the kind that replenishes us—isn't passive consumption. It's active restoration. It's making a cup of tea, lying on the floor with no agenda, reading a book, creating something with our hands, or sharing laughter with friends. Or maybe, it’s practicing yin yoga. Rest, at its best, reconnects us with ourselves.

In a world that equates speed with success, I’ve found that slowing down can actually be one of the most efficient choices I make. I'd consider myself an ambitious person, I like having goals and working towards things, but I'm not compromising my timeline by taking time to slow down. For me, a slow yoga practice is a reminder to take one step at a time, to notice the details, and to trust my ability to put in the effort when it is next required. 

Our world is now biased towards optimization and productivity. Our choices are weighed against a matrix of how much time we can save and how much we get out of it. Rest has become an afterthought if it's not directly advancing us towards some higher, more valuable end goal. Yet, in a world where we judge our value based on how much we produce it is rebellious to take time exclusively for rest. When we give ourselves the time and space to slow down things get quiet enough for us to listen more fully. To notice the details of how life is unfolding through our body. 

Yin yoga offers me the rare opportunity to witness my body and mind by getting quiet enough to listen. In that quiet, I am able to observe how tension shifts - a gentle dance between subtlety and intensity. It's a subversion of hustle culture, not through inaction, but through presence.
